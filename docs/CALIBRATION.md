# Lepro TB1 Physical Calibration

How the lamp's three concentric LED rings map from the d50 protocol's 196-LED
linear address space to physical clock positions. Worked out empirically on
2026-05-29 by sending single-LED markers and reading off where they appeared
on the actual lamp.

## Ring partition (re-confirmed)

The d50 protocol partitions 196 LEDs into three contiguous bands, and these
do correspond to the three physical rings:

| Ring | LED indices | Count | Physical position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outer | 0..87 | 88 | Outermost (largest diameter) |
| Middle | 88..149 | 62 | Middle ring |
| Inner | 150..195 | 46 | Innermost (smallest) |

This was already known from prior decoding but is now re-confirmed by
single-LED tests: lighting LED 0 alone shows on the outer ring, LED 88 alone
on the middle, LED 150 alone on the inner.

## Winding direction

**All three rings wind clockwise** from the strip's entry point. Verified by
testing LED N and LED N+k pairs and checking which direction the marker
moved. E.g., LED 0 at 8 o'clock and LED 87 at ~7 o'clock confirmed outer is
CW (LED 87 is *just before* LED 0, closing the loop).

## Ring start positions ("where is LED index 0 of each ring?")

The strip enters each ring at a different physical angle:

| Ring | First LED (absolute index) | Physical clock position | Angle (CW from 12) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outer | 0 | ~8 o'clock | 240° |
| Middle | 88 | ~8 o'clock | 240° |
| Inner | 150 | ~11 o'clock | 330° |

Outer and middle start at the same angle (likely the same physical entry
point on the lamp's frame); inner starts ~90° later, probably because the
strip routes through a different gap to reach the inner ring.

These positions are approximate — readings were eyeballed to the nearest
clock hour. Fine-tuning happens by adjusting the rotation constants below.

## Page → Physical rotation

The DIY page (and the ticker UI) draws each ring's "segment 0" at 12 o'clock
on screen (`a0 = -π/2`) and increments clockwise. To paint what the user
sees at the top of the screen onto the LED that's physically at the top of
the lamp, we rotate page-space LED indices into physical-space:

```
physical_index = (page_index + ring_offset) % ring_size
```

The offset for each ring is the number of LEDs CW from the ring's start
position to 12 o'clock:

```
offset = (degrees_from_12_to_ring_start_CCW * ring_size) / 360
```

| Ring | Start | Shift to 12 | LEDs/degree | Computed offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outer | 8 o'clock | 120° CW | 88/360 = 0.244 | **29** |
| Middle | 8 o'clock | 120° CW | 62/360 = 0.172 | **21** |
| Inner | 11 o'clock | 30° CW | 46/360 = 0.128 | **4** |

The middle ring (offset 21) verified dead-on at 12 o'clock in the
calibration test. Outer (29) and inner (4) verified within ~15° of perfect;
empirical fine-tuning likely lands at 31-33 for outer and 5-7 for inner.

## Diffuser physics worth knowing

Two visual quirks that make single-LED tests harder to read than they
should be:

1. **Lateral bleed** — a single lit LED in a tube diffuser appears as a
   ~3-LED-wide colored band, not a sharp point. Adjacent unlit LEDs glow
   from the spillover.

2. **Additive cross-ring mixing** — when LEDs on different rings light up
   close to each other angularly, their light bleeds through the diffusers
   and adds. Red on outer + green on middle at the same clock position will
   show a yellow-orange band on both rings. The first calibration attempt
   mistook this for a single ring lighting up with multiple colors — it was
   actually two rings, each with one LED, mixing through the diffuser.

Practical consequence: when calibrating, test ONE LED on ONE ring at a time,
not multiple markers across rings at once.

## How to apply the rotation in code

The cleanest place is at the page→protocol boundary in `workshop.py` — i.e.,
just before `build_d50_from_leds` is called by `api_diy_paint` and
`api_diy_save`. A `apply_lamp_rotation(page_leds) -> physical_leds` helper
keeps the d50 encoder itself protocol-pure.

The ticker's `build_ticker_d50` paints whole rings in single colors, so
rotation has no visible effect there and the call site can stay unchanged.

## Open questions

- The rings might wind at slightly different effective angles per LED if the
  diffuser tubes aren't exactly circular. The offsets here assume uniform
  angular spacing, which holds well enough for general painting but might
  matter for clock-face-style precision drawing.
- Captured presets from the official app use the lamp's native (physical)
  orientation already — they were generated by code that knows the physical
  layout. When loading our own DIY-saved presets, we must save them
  *post-rotation* (already in physical space) so they round-trip cleanly
  alongside the captured presets.
